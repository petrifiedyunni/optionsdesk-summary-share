Name filters

Fast first pass. A name must look tradeable and not already breaking down before it even gets a score.

Layer Rule Why
Pre-filter Pass ≥ 3 of 5 Cheap screen so weak / dead names never hit the full scorecard.
Price > $5 · volume spike > 1.2× avg · not down > 8% day-on-day · minimum absolute volume · positive close Wants participation today, not a falling knife or a penny name.
Liquidity 20-day avg volume ≥ 500k · price ≥ $10 · dollar volume ≥ $5m Swing size has to be able to enter and exit without owning the tape.

Risk, stop, and exits

Layer Rule Why
Stop max(EMA21, price − 1.5 ATR) Uses trend structure and volatility, not a fixed % stop.
Targets T1 = 1.5R · T2 = 2.5R Scale into strength instead of hoping for a home run.
Managed exits Sell 33% at 1R, 33% at 2R, trail the rest Locks some gain early; leftover rides with a trailing stop.

Sizing

Shares come from how much we are willing to lose at the stop — not from “I like this name.” Same sizer is used in backtest and the dry-run engine.

Step Rule Why
1. Risk budget ⅛ Kelly on trailing win-rate / avg win / avg loss · shrink toward 41% WR / 2.2R / 1.0R when history is thin Full Kelly is too aggressive. Eighth-Kelly is the safety factor; priors stop tiny samples from exploding size.
2. Hard ceiling Risk ≤ 2.5% of NAV × conviction tier (tier 1.0 → 2.5%, never above ~5%) Even a fat Kelly number cannot put a huge chunk of the account on one stop.
3. Convert to shares shares = floor( NAV × risk_frac / |entry − stop| ) A tighter stop → more shares; a wider stop → fewer. Two “Strong Buy” names do not get the same size.
4. Correlation cap Sector gross ≤ 25% NAV · factor (e.g. high-beta / semis) ≤ 35% NAV · shrink, don’t just reject Stops “5 trades” that are really one tech bet.
5. Portfolio vol Scale gross to ~15% annualized vol · scalar clamped 0.25–1.0 When the whole book is already hot, every new ticket gets smaller automatically.
6. Sentiment haircut Multiply shares by news/sentiment multiplier (0–1) Ugly headline → size down or skip, without changing the signal itself.
Worked example
NAV $8,000 · entry $100 · stop $94 (stop distance $6) · risk_frac 2.5%
Risk dollars = 8,000 × 0.025 = $200
Shares = floor(200 / 6) = 33 shares (~$3,300 notional). If that $200 stop-loss would push semis above 25% NAV, shares shrink until it fits.

OptionsDesk CSP Live · v2 VRP gate — this update

Purpose
  • Sell cash-secured puts when IV rank is elevated and IV is rich relative to realized vol
  • SPY regime gate blocks hostile tape
  • Frozen hypothesis — retune = new version
  • Telegram auto-approve optional; STOP / PAUSE / PANIC always on
What changed this update
Entry gate went from IVR-only to IVR + a cross-sectional vol-risk-premium (IV − realized vol) percentile + a vol-expansion cap — backtested to meaningfully improve risk-adjusted return over the IVR-only version, at the cost of trading less often. Screened universe went 100 → 300 names so the stricter gate still finds a reasonable number of candidates per day. Also: a real per-contract commission was found completely unmodeled in the backtest engine (now fixed) and a client-id collision bug that was silently degrading live scan data (regime, option chain, capital checks) whenever another desk process was already connected (now fixed).

Name-level filters

A ticker must clear all of these before we even look at a strike. Fail any one → skip.

Layer Rule Why
Universe Top 300 US names by average volume Only names that can actually fill a short put without owning the book. Widened this update so a stricter gate still surfaces enough candidates daily.
Volatility IV Rank ≥ 80 IV Rank = where today’s implied vol sits vs the last year. Raised from 70 this update — the tighter floor is what the VRP backtest showed actually carries edge.
Vol risk premium new Cross-sectional VRP percentile ≥ 95 Vol expansion < 1.2 Passing the IVR floor isn’t enough on its own — some IVR pops aren’t backed by real relative richness. VRP ranks today’s candidates against each other on (IV − realized vol); vol-expansion filters out names where realized vol is actively accelerating (RV10/RV60), since that’s a regime where short vol gets hurt.
Earnings No earnings within 5 days Binary jump risk. That window belongs to Sleeve E, not this CSP book.
Chain Option chain must exist No expiry / strike data → nothing to sell.
Quote quality Skip if no live bid/ask After-hours empty quotes used to look like fills. Now they are rejected at build time.
Regime Bull = full size Range = reduced Bear / high-vol chop = stand down Short-vol loses when the tape is trending hard against you. V2 uses SPY ADX + VIX percentile, not a blunt VIX on/off switch.
Capital Full CSP collateral available Strike × 100 must fit inside cash + sleeve cap. No naked leverage here.
Dedupe One underlying at a time Blocks a second CSP if we already hold that name — even if delta targeting drifts to a nearby strike (the CPNG $15 vs $15.50 case).

Contract selection

Once the name passes, we pick one cash-secured put. All of these must match hypothesis v2.

Layer Rule Why
Structure Short cash-secured put Defined collateral, known max loss = strike × 100 − credit.
Delta Target ~0.25 · accept 0.20–0.30 Far enough OTM to win often, close enough that the premium is still worth selling.
Tenor 7–14 DTE · target ~10 Theta decays fast here without sitting through a full month of gap risk.
Spread Bid/ask ≤ 20% of mid Wide markets eat the edge on entry and exit.

Exits

Auto-close only fires on these marks. It will not unwind a “shouldn’t have opened” trade.

Layer Rule Why
Take profit Buy back when ask ≤ 50% of credit received Capture most of the premium early; leftover theta isn’t worth the tail risk.
Stop Buy back when ask ≥ 2× credit Premium doubled against us → cut before assignment risk explodes.
Mark Use live ask (what we’d pay to close) Last/mid at the open caused phantom profit triggers.

Sizing

Contracts come from collateral budget, then cash + sleeve must actually fit. Same builder in scan and live.

Step Rule Why
1. Collateral budget budget = NAV × 5% × regime scalar (scalar 0–1 from SPY ADX + VIX) Bull = full size. Range = haircut. Bear / structural halt = 0 (stand down).
2. Convert to lots contracts = floor(budget / (strike × 100)) · floor at 1 lot Higher strike → fewer lots. Small NAV almost always prints 1 lot, not a fractional put.
3. Cash + sleeve check Need strike × 100 × contracts in cash and under the 80% NAV sleeve cap. If not, retry 1 lot; still no → skip Never sell a put we cannot fully cash-secure, and never let options eat the whole account.
4. Book caps Max 5 open · max 15 new / week · one ticker at a time v2 raised weekly (was 2) and sleeve % (was 40%) so the 3/day session isn’t fake-blocked. Ticker dedupe stops the CPNG double.
Worked example
NAV $8,000 · $20 strike CSP · bull scalar 1.0
Budget = 8,000 × 0.05 × 1.0 = $400
Collateral / lot = 20 × 100 = $2,000 → floor(400 / 2,000) = 0 → 1 lot ($2,000 collateral) if cash + sleeve (80% = $6,400) still have room; otherwise skip.
Same put at $80k NAV → floor(4,000 / 2,000) = 2 lots. Range scalar 0.5 would cut that to 1.

Sleeve E · Earnings IV Crush Live

Purpose
  • Own the 1–3 DTE earnings window that CSP explicitly skips
  • Default structure is a defined-risk vertical (put credit if bullish, call credit if bearish)
  • Naked CSP only with explicit Telegram YES NAKED when implied move is small
  • Unconditional morning-after close to harvest crush

When a name qualifies

This sleeve only looks at names about to report. CSP is explicitly banned from the same window.

Layer Rule Why
Universe Top 200 liquid US names, or a pinned watchlist Earnings moves need a deep enough chain to buy the long wing.
Calendar 1–3 days to earnings Too early → crush hasn’t priced in yet. Day-of / after → already too late.
IV Rank IVR ≥ 65 Slightly lower bar than CSP (70) because event vol is usually already rich.
Implied move Skip if straddle move > 25% Fat-tail names can gap through any wing. Don’t sell those events.

Structure

Default is defined-risk. Naked is opt-in only, and only when the implied move is small.

Layer Rule Why
Default Credit vertical — put spread if bullish, call spread if bearish Max loss is the width minus credit. Assignment on the short leg is hedged by the long wing.
Short delta ~0.22 A bit further OTM than CSP’s 0.25 — earnings gaps are wider than normal days.
Wing ~8% away from the short strike Cheap defined-risk hedge; keeps max loss inside the 2% NAV budget.
Naked override Only if implied move < 15% and Telegram YES NAKED Small expected gap → CSP-style premium can be worth it, but a human has to say so.

Exits

Layer Rule Why
In session Take profit 50% of credit · stop at 2× Same discipline as CSP before the print.
Morning after Close at 30% of credit (looser) or market — don’t hold a second session The crush usually happens overnight. Sitting another day just reloads gap risk.

Sizing

Spreads size off max loss. Naked sizes off collateral, same as a CSP. Both clamp 1–5 lots.

Step Rule Why
1. Spread max loss max_loss / lot = (wing width − net credit) × 100 Defined-risk: the worst case is known before we hit send.
2. Spread budget risk $ = NAV × 2% · contracts = floor(risk $ / max_loss) One earnings name can take at most ~2% of the account if the print goes the wrong way.
3. Naked (opt-in) max collateral = min(NAV × 5%, sleeve remaining) · contracts = floor(that / (K × 100)) Same per-name collateral cap as CSP. Only after implied move < 15% and a YES NAKED.
4. Hard clamp Always 1–5 contracts, regardless of NAV Small accounts floor at 1 lot (may risk a bit over 2%). Fat NAV still cannot dump 20 lots into one print.
Worked example
NAV $8,000 · $30 / $27.50 put credit $0.40 (width $2.50)
Max loss / lot = (2.50 − 0.40) × 100 = $210
Risk budget = 8,000 × 0.02 = $160 → floor(160 / 210) = 0 → 1 lot (total max loss $210 ≈ 2.6% NAV).
Same spread at $50k NAV → floor(1,000 / 210) = 4 lots ($840 max loss). Naked $30 put at $50k → min($2,500, sleeve left) / $3,000 → 1 lot if the sleeve still has room.

Sleeve D · 0DTE SPY Iron Condor Paper · new this update

Purpose
  • Sell same-day-expiry SPY premium with a defined-risk structure — never a naked short
  • Lives in VOLHARVEST (desk/), not optionsdesk/ — needs atomic 4-leg execution (single BAG order) that the CSP executor doesn't have
  • Entry window and exit rule are both backtest-validated, not assumed
  • Exit safety runs as its own standalone process, separate from entry — same design principle as the ExpirationGuard assignment-prevention service
Why 0DTE and why now
Ran a sandbox backtest first (2 years of 15-min SPY bars, Black-Scholes priced off a daily realized-vol proxy) before writing any live code. Two things it found: entry time of day matters a lot once wing width is properly scaled to the day's remaining expected move (not a fixed dollar width — that overstated the effect on a first pass), and a stop-loss helps a lot for morning entries but a profit-target actually hurts in the same window — it cuts winners short faster than it reduces risk.

When it trades

SPY only, one attempt per day. Fail either of these → the Risk Officer hard-rejects, no override.

Layer Rule Why
Entry window 09:45–13:30 ET only The only window actually backtested. 14:30+ entries were tested and were meaningfully worse; nothing before 09:45 was tested at all — the Risk Officer rejects outside this window rather than silently allowing an unvalidated time.
Cadence Max 1 entry / day Same-day risk budget discipline — one shot, not a scan loop re-entering all window long.
Strikes Short legs at ~16-delta both sides, symmetric A conventional, well-understood OTM target for a same-day structure.
Wing width Scaled to the day's remaining expected move (spot × σ × √T), not fixed A fixed-dollar wing tested first and made shorter entries look better than they really were, purely mechanically — this scaling removed that artifact.

Exits — owned by a separate standalone process

zero_dte_guard.py re-derives truth from the broker every cycle — it does not trust the entry process's in-memory state, so a crash/hang in entry doesn't strand a position unmonitored.

Layer Rule Why
Stop-loss Buy back at 2× credit Backtested best rule for the validated entry window — kept ~all the mean return of holding to close while cutting the worst trade meaningfully.
Profit target None — deliberate Tested and it actively hurt risk-adjusted return here. Not an oversight — removing this note without re-running the backtest would be a regression.
EOD flatten Force-close by 15:50 ET, unconditional 0DTE options are American-style and physically settled — never let one ride into the close regardless of P&L.
Mark Live bid/ask on all 4 legs — pay ask to buy back shorts, receive bid to sell back longs No live quote on any leg → skip the cycle rather than guess.

Sizing & Risk Officer

Same Risk Officer that hard-bans naked/unbounded-loss structures for every VOLHARVEST sleeve — this one just also gets two extra 0DTE-specific checks.

Step Rule Why
1. Risk tier 1% of NAV — the most conservative tier of any sleeve New, never-live-tested structure with real tail risk even net of the stop-loss.
2. Contracts contracts = floor(risk budget / max loss per contract), floored at 0 Standard defined-risk sizing — if the budget can't cover one contract's max loss, it sizes to zero rather than rounding up.
3. Structural checks Must be defined-risk (finite max loss, ≥1 long + ≥1 short leg) — same as every sleeve StructureType.IRON_CONDOR already existed in the Risk Officer's allowed set; nothing new needed there.
Real run, live data (this update)
SPY spot $769.35 · daily-vol proxy 12.5% · condor priced ~$4 wings, credit $0.62/share, max loss $338/contract · real account NAV $8,430 (via IB NetLiq) → 1% risk budget = $84. Risk Officer correctly rejects — budget can't cover one contract yet. Same account-size story as the CSP sleeve; not a bug, a real current constraint.

Safety design

Execution
  • Single atomic BAG order for all 4 legs — never legs in, so no partial-fill leaves naked exposure
  • Laddered limit price, 3 steps, cancels rather than chasing indefinitely
  • Paper only right now — VOLHARVEST's own live-trading gate stays locked for this sleeve
A bug this caught before it shipped
  • The existing assignment-prevention guard force-closes any short leg within 1 DTE — but a 0DTE leg's DTE is always 0, so unmodified it would've force-closed this sleeve's position on its first check after entry
  • Fixed by excluding this sleeve's open legs from that guard's view — this sleeve's own guard fully owns the same-day-close guarantee instead

Architecture

Design
  • Not microservices — one process family sharing IBKR + Telegram
  • Desks are packages with hard risk boundaries and separate clientIds
  • Sleeve E is the live earnings path; CSP skips that window (±5d)
  • Paper vs live is coded, not just convention
  • Safety-critical intraday checks are their own standalone process, deliberately not sharing fate with the entry loop — ExpirationGuard (assignment prevention) and Sleeve D's guard (stop-loss + EOD flatten) both follow this
